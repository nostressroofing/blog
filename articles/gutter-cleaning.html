When to Clean Your Gutters in Saskatoon (and Why Twice Matters)

Saskatoon roofing advice · Gutters

Most people think of gutter cleaning as a once-a-year chore, usually in the fall. In Saskatoon, once isn't enough. Homeowners who clean twice a year — spring and fall — have dramatically fewer problems than the once-a-year crowd. Here's why, and when to book each one.

Fall: before the freeze

The fall cleaning is the one everyone knows about. Leaves, shingle grit, and summer debris come out before the first hard freeze — usually sometime in October here. A gutter full of wet leaves that freezes solid becomes a trough of ice sitting against your fascia all winter. When spring melt comes, that ice forces water behind the gutter and into your soffits instead of down the downspout where it belongs.

Timing matters: too early and the last of the leaves fall after you've cleaned; too late and you're chipping ice. Late October is the sweet spot in most years.

Spring: the one people skip

The spring cleaning — late April or May, after the snow is gone — is the one that actually saves foundations. All winter, grit washes off your shingles and settles in the gutters. Come spring melt and those heavy May rains, clogged gutters overflow right at the foundation line. In Saskatoon's clay-heavy soil, that water doesn't drain away; it sits against the basement wall.

The result shows up every June: damp basements, with homeowners blaming their weeping tile when the real culprit was a skipped gutter cleaning.

What clogged gutters actually cost

  • Fascia rot. Water sitting in a clogged gutter soaks the fascia board behind it. Replacing rotted fascia runs into the thousands.
  • Foundation water. Overflowing gutters dump water exactly where you don't want it. Basement waterproofing starts around $10,000.
  • Ice dams. Clogged gutters in fall mean standing water that freezes, giving ice dams a head start in January.
  • Gutter damage. A gutter full of ice is enormously heavy — heavy enough to pull the gutter away from the house.

Can you do it yourself?

Honestly, yes, if you're comfortable on a ladder and your house is a single storey. Scoop the debris, flush with a hose, check that downspouts run clear. But two-storey homes, steep lots, or gutters you can't safely reach are a different story — that's the most common reason people hire a professional, and there's no shame in it. A professional cleaning also spots the early stuff: loose hangers, separated seams, downspouts draining toward the foundation.
